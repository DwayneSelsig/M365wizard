# SharePoint

SharePoint helps people organize, manage, and publish information for its intended audience. Sites, pages, lists, libraries, and permissions give that information a clear home and owner. The audience can be a working Team, a department, or the whole organization.

## Best For

Use SharePoint for intranet content, document libraries, published information, structured lists, team sites, and organization resources. Teams can use a SharePoint site for shared content, but SharePoint also serves people who are not members of that Team.

## Be Careful With

SharePoint can become messy when ownership is unclear. Every important site, library, list, and page needs an owner, a purpose, and a lifecycle. Do not use a homepage to copy content that already has a managed source.

## Permissions: Prefer Inheritance

Let libraries, lists, pages, and items inherit their permissions from the site whenever possible. This gives people a predictable experience and lets owners review access in one place. Break inheritance only for a documented, genuinely different audience, with a named owner who reviews that exception.

## Add Purview For Information Policy

SharePoint provides the sites, libraries, metadata, versions, and permissions used to manage documents. When information also needs organization-wide classification, protection, retention, records, investigation, or compliance controls, add [Microsoft Purview](../purview.md). Use [Which Microsoft Purview Solution Should You Use?](../../decisions/which-purview-solution-should-you-use.md) to select the control without treating Purview as a replacement for clear SharePoint structure and ownership.
